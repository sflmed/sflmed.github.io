Five Everyday Habits for Healthier Leg Circulation

October 27, 2026

Moving your legs helps blood keep flowing, and a few habits are backed by guidance from US government health agencies. This post sticks to what the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) and MedlinePlus say.

1. Move often when you have been sitting a long time

The CDC lists sitting for a long time, especially with crossed legs, among the things that slow blood flow. For long trips, its travel guidance says to move your legs frequently and walk around every 1 to 2 hours.

2. Flex your ankles when you cannot get up

The CDC's travel guidance also says to exercise your calf muscles to improve the flow of blood. One of its suggestions is to extend your legs straight out and flex your ankles by pulling your toes toward you.

3. Walk regularly

The NHLBI describes exercise programs as part of treatment for peripheral artery disease (PAD). Its goal for these programs is to work up to at least 30 minutes of continuous walking. Your provider may recommend a supervised program in a clinic or hospital, or a home-based program with coaching. Ask your provider which fits you.

4. If you smoke, get help quitting

The NHLBI says smoking is the main risk factor of PAD, and that quitting can reduce your symptoms and your risk of complications. It also advises avoiding secondhand smoke and points readers to the National Cancer Institute's Smoking Quitline for free help.

5. Keep swollen legs above heart level when you lie down

MedlinePlus, from the National Library of Medicine, says to keep your legs above your heart level when lying down, if possible, so the fluid can drain. It says not to do this if you get shortness of breath, and to see your provider instead. It also says to contact your provider about any unexplained swelling.

When to ask about leg symptoms

The NHLBI describes leg pain that comes on when walking or climbing stairs and goes away after rest as intermittent claudication. It says that without treatment, PAD may cause sores, infections, and even the loss of a limb. The NHLBI also says your risk of PAD goes up as you get older and lists high blood pressure and diabetes among the medical conditions that raise it. If you have this kind of leg pain, tell a clinician.

Swelling, pain or tenderness, warmth, or redness in an affected leg can be signs of a blood clot called deep vein thrombosis (DVT). The CDC says to see your doctor as soon as possible if you have any of these signs.
